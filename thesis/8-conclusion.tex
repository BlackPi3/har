\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{chap:conclusion}

This thesis investigated whether adversarial training can help close the domain gap between simulated and real accelerometer signals in Human Activity Recognition \cite{lara2012survey, hammerla2016deep}. Building on an end-to-end framework that jointly trains a pose-to-IMU regressor and an activity classifier \cite{zolfaghari2024sensor}, we systematically evaluated eight training scenarios, including loss ablations, architectural variants, auxiliary pose data, and two adversarial approaches, across UTD-MHAD \cite{chen2015utd} and MM-Fit \cite{stromback2020mm}, optimized through a three-pass hyperparameter search and evaluated over multiple random seeds.

Signal-level adversarial training with WGAN-GP \cite{arjovsky2017wasserstein, gulrajani2017improved} achieved the best result in this work, improving macro F1 on UTD-MHAD by 3.3\% over the baseline. The discriminator, operating on raw accelerometer signals, encouraged the regressor to produce outputs whose distribution more closely matched real data, complementing the point-wise MSE loss with a distributional alignment objective. Feature-level adversarial training with GRL \cite{ganin2016domain}, by contrast, achieved domain confusion at the representation level but degraded classification on both datasets, suggesting that enforcing domain invariance in the learned feature space may discard information that is both domain-specific and task-relevant. However, the signal-level improvement did not transfer to MM-Fit, where the baseline already achieved the highest F1 and all variants.

This divergence between datasets was the most consistent pattern across all experiments and probably reflects the role of dataset size. On UTD-MHAD \cite{chen2015utd}, with limited training subjects, the additional training signals from adversarial and other objectives appear to provide meaningful regularization. On MM-Fit \cite{stromback2020mm}, with more training data, the baseline is likely already near-optimal, and auxiliary objectives, including adversarial gradients, may push the model away from that optimum rather than improving upon it. The ablation studies reinforced that the regression loss is a necessary anchor for the entire framework, and that auxiliary pose data from NTU RGB+D \cite{shahroudy2016ntu} helps primarily when domain compatibility with the target dataset is sufficient.

The findings of this thesis suggest that adversarial training can improve simulation-augmented HAR, but its benefit appears conditional on the data regime. In the case of UTD-MHAD, signal-level adversarial training offers a promising direction. Whether this approach generalizes beyond the datasets studied here, and whether alternative adversarial formulations could extend its benefits to larger datasets, remain open questions for future work.
